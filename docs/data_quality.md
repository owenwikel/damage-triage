## 1. Invalid polygons

| split | tile | uid | damage | problem | vertices | area_px | make_valid | buffer(0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | hurricane-michael_00000172 | 2aaab43d-9165-49eb-8dee-a16d86f40e57 | no-damage | Self-intersection[807.0085575281 581.346745336051] | 11 | 2,582.7 | GeometryCollection, area 2582.7 | Polygon, area 2582.7 |

`problem` is shapely's `explain_validity`. A self-intersection means the outline crosses itself (a 'bow-tie'), usually a digitising slip where a vertex was placed on the wrong side of an edge. `make_valid` and `buffer(0)` are the two standard repairs; their resulting type and area are shown.

**For modelling:** one polygon out of the whole set can't move any metric, but it can crash code that assumes valid geometry (area, intersection, rasterising with some libraries). Suggested handling: repair with `buffer(0)` (keeps a single Polygon when the area is unchanged) or `make_valid` and keep the largest polygon part, or drop that one building. Whichever is chosen, do it in one place (the label loader) so every downstream step sees the same geometry.

Coordinate range over all polygons: x -0.36 to 1024.04, y -0.00 to 1024.00; 3,439 polygons extend slightly outside the 0-1024 image. The overshoot is under half a pixel, so clipping to the image bounds changes nothing visible, but code that indexes pixels from polygon coordinates should clip first.

## 6. Pre/post image misalignment

Estimated for 200 tiles: 20 per disaster, sampled at random (seed 0) from all tiles, including ones without buildings. Method (`src/alignment.py`): each tile is split into overlapping 128x128 patches; patches with no-data or no texture are skipped; each patch's shift is found with low-pass phase correlation, searched within +-16 px; the tile's shift is the median over its patches. On synthetic tests the method recovers known shifts exactly (`tests/test_alignment.py`).

`median/p90/max px` = size of the tile's (median-of-patches) shift. `median patch agreement` = share of a tile's patches within 1.5 px of the tile's shift. `global est.` = a single phase correlation over the whole tile with no search limit, as a cross-check; the last column counts tiles where it lands beyond +-16 px.

**Null control: how much of this is noise?** The same estimator was run on 50 *unrelated* pairs (one tile's pre image with a different tile's post image), where there is no true shift to find. Whatever it reports there is the method's noise floor:

| input | median shift px | p75 shift px | p90 shift px | median patch agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| real pre/post pairs (n=200) | 2.91 | 4.03 | 5.66 | 0.10 |
| unrelated pairs, null control (n=50) | 2.06 | 2.92 | 3.40 | 0.01 |

**How far to trust this.** The shift sizes for real pairs are barely above the null control, so **this method cannot measure the misalignment**; the per-disaster 'median px' values above are mostly its noise floor, not a real offset. What the data does support:

- there *is* some shared structure: patch agreement is higher on real pairs (0.10) than on unrelated ones (0.01), so the images are related, but most patches still don't agree with each other;
- the tile-level estimates are not large: they stay within a few pixels of the noise floor, and none of the other methods tried during development (whole-tile phase correlation on intensity and gradient images, with and without low-pass filtering) gave consistent large shifts either. The unbounded whole-tile estimates beyond +-16 px in the table are spurious peaks, and they don't agree with the patch estimates for the same tile.

Why most patches disagree can't be settled from this: the shift could vary across a tile (parallax, since the two captures were taken from different viewing angles and taller buildings move more than the ground), or the two captures could simply differ too much (lighting, season, sensor, damage) for patch matching to work. A more direct measurement would need hand-marked matching points on a few tiles, or a feature-matching method (e.g. SIFT/ORB with outlier rejection), which needs a library not currently installed (OpenCV). Per-tile values: `outputs/audit/shift_estimates.csv` and `outputs/audit/shift_null_control.csv`.

**For modelling:** misalignment between pre and post images is unmeasured, not known to be zero. If it's a few pixels, it's small next to a typical building (median footprint ~650 px², roughly 25x25 px) but large for the tiniest ones (section 3). Inputs are affected differently:

- crops cut tightly to the polygon may clip the post-image building; context padding reduces that (see the crop-size section);
- a pixel-difference input (post minus pre) turns any misalignment into false 'change' along edges, even for undamaged buildings;
- channel stacking and siamese features are more tolerant of a few pixels of shift, because convolution and pooling blur small offsets.

Visual inspection of the tile figures in `outputs/viz/` is currently the best check available: outlines drawn from the same polygon on both images can be compared by eye against the buildings.

Two things are visible in the example crops (judged by eye, not measured). First, the polygon lines up with the building in the pre image, but in some post images the building sits several pixels off it: about 10 px for the Palu example, a few pixels for the Michael and Florence ones. That is direct visual evidence of the pre/post misalignment that section 6 could not measure. Second, the Mexico example's post image is much blurrier than its pre image, so the two captures can differ in effective resolution.

**For modelling:** the median building is 36 px on its longest side, so a 64 px crop with 16 px of context fits 42% of buildings, and 128 px with 32 px fits 89%. Small crops force a choice between shrinking larger buildings (losing detail) and cutting them off. Large crops leave small buildings as a few pixels in a mostly-context window, or require upsampling. Classes differ in size (destroyed buildings are the smallest, major-damage the largest; section 3), so the crop setting changes each class's input differently. Context padding would also absorb a few pixels of pre/post misalignment (visible above) and gives the model surroundings (debris, water, burn scars), which the observations suggest carry damage information. Larger crops cost GPU memory quadratically: 128 px is 4x the pixels of 64 px.
