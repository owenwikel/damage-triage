## Would a tile-level split within a disaster leak?

The `val-tiles` idea splits training disasters by tile, so neighbouring tiles can end up in different parts. Two checks:

**1. Buildings near the tile border.** A tile whose buildings sit close to its edge probably has neighbours showing the same street (and, for buildings cut by the edge, part of the same building).

| disaster | tiles with buildings | tiles with a building < 50 px from border | buildings | buildings < 50 px from border | share of tiles | share of buildings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| guatemala-volcano | 22 | 18 | 888 | 217 | 81.8% | 24.4% |
| hurricane-florence | 409 | 341 | 8,714 | 2,362 | 83.4% | 27.1% |
| hurricane-harvey | 399 | 351 | 30,729 | 9,437 | 88.0% | 30.7% |
| hurricane-matthew | 290 | 268 | 18,128 | 4,660 | 92.4% | 25.7% |
| hurricane-michael | 427 | 413 | 28,343 | 8,154 | 96.7% | 28.8% |
| mexico-earthquake | 159 | 159 | 43,682 | 12,702 | 100.0% | 29.1% |
| midwest-flooding | 318 | 261 | 11,288 | 3,047 | 82.1% | 27.0% |
| palu-tsunami | 153 | 152 | 43,954 | 11,002 | 99.3% | 25.0% |
| santa-rosa-wildfire | 291 | 274 | 17,176 | 4,816 | 94.2% | 28.0% |
| socal-fire | 567 | 502 | 14,747 | 4,474 | 88.5% | 30.3% |
| all | 3,035 | 2,739 | 217,649 | 60,871 | 90.2% | 28.0% |

**2. The same building in two tiles.** Each building's longitude/latitude centroid was snapped to a ~1 m grid; a grid cell holding buildings from two different tiles of the same disaster means the tiles overlap on the ground.

None found: no building appears whole in two tiles. This catches overlapping tiles, but not a building cut by a shared border: its two partial outlines have different centroids.

**What this means:** almost every tile has buildings near its border, so most tiles have neighbours with shared context (same streets, same kind of damage; damage is spatially clustered, see `docs/observations.md`). No building appears whole in two tiles, so tiles don't seem to overlap, but a val-tiles score will likely read optimistic compared with a new disaster. That is the point of reporting it next to the disaster-held-out val set: the gap between the two measures how much the model relies on familiar context. Grouping neighbouring tiles together (e.g. by lng/lat blocks) before splitting would reduce the leak.
